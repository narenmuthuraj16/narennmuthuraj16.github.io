Project 01: Closed-Loop Electromechanical Exhaust Controller

Embedded Systems Mechatronics Control Logic Fabrication

Fabricated a switchable automotive exhaust bypass system to maximize volumetric efficiency, upcycling OEM steel piping via TIG welding for thermal endurance[cite: 4]. The core achievement was architecting an Arduino/L298 closed-loop system utilizing an ACS712 current sensor to intelligently detect mechanical stalls and cut motor power to prevent burnout[cite: 4].

Microcontroller Arduino Uno Rev 3
Power Delivery L298 H-bridge (12V)
Feedback Loop ACS712 Hall-Effect

System Architecture & Signal Processing

  • Replaced failure-prone mechanical limit switches by programming Exponential Moving Average (EMA) filtering and rate-of-change (dI/dt) logic in C++ to instantly cut motor power upon detecting the current spike of a mechanical stall at the valve's end-of-travel[cite: 4, 5].
  • Engineered a transient torque-boost startup sequence, injecting a full 255 PWM pulse for exactly 165 ms to shatter static friction before settling to a 240 PWM operating baseline[cite: 3].
  • Hardcoded a 50 ms software debounce delay to filter micro-millisecond signal bounces caused by the physical inconsistency of human button presses during operation[cite: 3].

Project 02: Automotive LIN Bus Architecture Retrofit & Protocol Translation

Network Topology Reverse Engineering Firmware Architecture Signal Injection

Conducted an architectural feasibility study and protocol translation design to integrate an MQB-platform steering wheel onto a legacy VW Polo PQ25 (6R/6C) chassis[cite: 5, 13]. The objective was to intercept, translate, and inject cruise control LIN frames across mismatched network topologies to enable full functionality from an aftermarket hardware installation[cite: 5, 13].

Bus Architecture Dual-LIN Transceiver Emulator
Microcontroller Arduino Pro Mini 5V
Network Nodes TJA1021 / MCP2004

The Interoperability Challenge

The MQB steering wheel utilizes a fundamentally different LIN dialect and network topology compared to the PQ25 combination switch[cite: 13]. Direct hardware installation fails because the legacy PQ25 J527 steering column module cannot interpret modern MQB cruise control frames[cite: 13].

Hardware Integration & System Logic

  • Dual-Network Isolation: Designed a bridging architecture that separates the networks using two individual TJA1021 (or MCP2004 equivalent) LIN transceivers—one connected to the MQB steering wheel, and the other to the PQ25 J527 stalk[cite: 13].
  • Data Sniffing & Translation: Programmed the Arduino Pro Mini to sniff the incoming MQB steering wheel LIN frames (specifically intercepting the SET, RES, +, –, and CANCEL commands)[cite: 13]. The logic then translates these specific packets into the older PQ25 stalk LIN format[cite: 13].
  • Signal Injection: The translated frames are continuously injected into the PQ25 cruise control LIN cluster, tricking the legacy Body Control Module (BCM) into recognizing native hardware[cite: 13].
  • Environmental Shielding: To operate reliably within a volatile automotive electrical environment, the design incorporates an automotive-grade 7805 linear regulator to step down the 12V ignition power to a stable, low-noise 5V supply, paired with optocoupler isolation to protect the microcontroller from voltage spikes[cite: 13].

Project 03: MO.EV.ES. Electric Mountain Cart Architecture

Chassis Prototyping Kinematics DFM Human-Centered Design

Developed during the Politecnico di Milano Design & Engineering studio, this project bridged human-centered design with heavy-duty mechanical constraints[cite: 1]. The objective was to engineer and physically prototype an electric-assisted, 4-wheeled cargo vehicle capable of transporting 200 kg payloads up 25° inclines[cite: 1, 3]. Serving as a last-mile resupply solution for off-road mountain refuge workers, the design required navigating industrial manufacturing procedures, powertrain integration, and extreme ergonomic environments[cite: 1].

Payload Capacity 200 kg @ 25° Incline
Primary Motor Motenergy 1117 (48V)
Structural Material AISI 304 Stainless Steel

Kinematics & Chassis Architecture

  • Passive Pivot Suspension: Bypassed complex and expensive Ackermann steering geometry and active suspension in favor of a robust, heavy-duty central-pivot wagon linkage[cite: 1]. This allowed the front axle to passively articulate over rocky, uneven alpine terrain while keeping manufacturing costs low[cite: 1].
  • Traction Dynamics: Analyzed multiple traction configurations (including tank tracks and solid foam) before selecting 15x6.00-6 pneumatic turf tires to optimize ground pressure for delicate alpine grass while maintaining essential grip[cite: 1].
  • Structural Design: Architected the primary tubular chassis using a hybrid designer-engineer approach, prioritizing load-bearing capabilities for the 200kg payload while minimizing the overall footprint for narrow mountain trails[cite: 1].

Powertrain & Systems Integration

  • Torque Multiplication: Tasked with coupling the provided high-voltage Motenergy 1117 motor and Sevcon Gen4 inverter to the wheels[cite: 1, 4]. Researched and integrated a commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) reduction gearbox to safely step down the motor RPM to the required 55-70 RPM wheel speed[cite: 1].
  • Slope Constraints: Ensured the selected gearbox multiplied torque sufficiently to achieve the >1100 N total pulling force required to prevent the cart from rolling backward on extreme 25° gradients[cite: 1].

Ergonomics & Human-Machine Interface (HMI)

  • Anthropometric Adjustability: Designed a vertically adjustable main push-handle based on ergonomic principles to accommodate the height variations and lifting mechanics of different refuge workers[cite: 1].
  • Tactile Control: Replaced standard motorcycle twist-grips (which pose a severe safety hazard if an operator stumbles on rocky terrain) with a gradual-input push-button throttle[cite: 1]. Integrated a traditional bicycle-style mechanical brake lever for intuitive, immediate stopping power during steep descents[cite: 1].

Design for Manufacturing (DFM) & Prototyping

  • Industrial Replication: Managed the physical 1:1 prototyping phase to strictly follow real-world industrial fabrication processes[cite: 1]. Constructed custom bending jigs and made specialized tools to manually form the aluminum and steel tubular chassis elements[cite: 1].
  • Component Fabrication: Utilized rapid 3D printing for non-load-bearing brackets and UI housings during testing[cite: 1]. Conceptually engineered the primary power box in PBT plastic for future injection molding, maintaining strict draft angles and wall thicknesses[cite: 1].
