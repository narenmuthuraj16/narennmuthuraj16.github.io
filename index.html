Closed-Loop Electromechanical Exhaust Controller

Embedded C++ Mechatronics TIG Welding Signal Processing

Designed and developed a custom Arduino-based control system for an automotive electronic exhaust cut-off valve to maximize engine volumetric efficiency through a scavenging effect[cite: 1]. The objective was to retrofit intelligent push-button control onto an existing electrically actuated exhaust flap while preventing mechanical over-travel and jamming at the valve's physical end-stops[cite: 1].

Microcontroller Arduino Uno Rev 3
Motor Driver L298 H-bridge (12V)
Sensor Integration ACS712 30A Hall-Effect

Mechanical Architecture & Thermal Management

  • Upcycled OEM-grade steel piping from scrap Volkswagen Jetta exhaust manifolds, utilizing TIG welding to withstand severe thermal cycling and optimize the scavenging effect[cite: 1].
  • Mounted the 12V automotive exhaust flap motor with a calculated spatial offset to decouple it from direct thermal conduction from the extreme radiant heat of the exhaust manifold[cite: 1].
  • Weather-proofed the actuator's factory plastic gearbox housing using a heat-shrink overmolding technique to establish a hermetic seal against water ingress and highly abrasive road debris[cite: 1].

Advanced Control Strategy & Signal Processing

  • Transitioned from a fragile, fixed-duration motor activation sequence to an adaptive, current-feedback closed-loop control system that monitors mechanical load dynamically[cite: 1].
  • Implemented an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) filter with an optimized smoothing factor of α = 0.20 to prevent false stall detections from the inherently noisy analog signal of the ACS712 sensor[cite: 1].
  • Calculated the rate-of-change (dI/dt) of the filtered current over 20 ms intervals to detect the mechanical end-of-travel, instantly cutting power via the L298 driver when the current spikes rapidly (exceeding a calculated rate of 0.32)[cite: 1].
  • Masked the natural inductive spike of motor startup inrush current by implementing an ignoreSpikeTime variable that pauses rate-of-change detection for the first 250 ms of operation[cite: 1].
  • Programmed independent threshold tracking for opening and closing parameters to compensate for asymmetric aerodynamic and mechanical loads presented by the pressurized exhaust flow[cite: 1].
  • Engineered a transient torque-boost startup sequence, injecting a full 255 PWM pulse for exactly 165 ms to shatter static friction before dropping to a continuous 240 PWM operating baseline[cite: 1].

Human-Machine Interface (HMI)

  • Reverse-engineered a factory-spec Volkswagen dashboard dummy button operating on an active-low input (INPUT_PULLUP) to maintain an OEM cabin aesthetic[cite: 1].
  • Hardcoded a 50 ms software debounce delay into the system to filter micro-millisecond signal bounces caused by the physical inconsistency of human button presses[cite: 1].
  • Interpreted user commands using a state-toggling logic loop based on previous position memory stored directly in the Arduino[cite: 1].
